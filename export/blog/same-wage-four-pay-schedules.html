Same wage, four different checks

Weekly, every two weeks, twice a month, and monthly change the size of the check, not the year.

A job that pays $20 an hour does not produce one check size. It produces four, depending on how often you are paid. The year is the same if you work full time all year. The envelope is not.

One wage, four gross checks

  • Weekly: 40 hours, $800 gross. About 52 checks.
  • Every two weeks: 80 hours, $1,600 gross. About 26 checks.
  • Twice a month: 86.67 hours, about $1,733 gross. 24 checks.
  • Monthly: 173.33 hours, about $3,467 gross. 12 checks.

Those four gross amounts are the same job. Each one is about $41,600 for the year. A monthly check looks large because it is holding a month of pay. A weekly check looks small because it is holding a week. Comparing a friend’s monthly deposit with your weekly deposit, without multiplying by how often you are paid, is the usual mix-up.

Take-home follows the check, not a new tax

The calculator annualizes whatever check you enter, then splits the year’s tax back onto that check. If the hours match the pay schedule, the yearly take-home barely moves. In this estimate, $20 an hour, single, Pennsylvania, no local tax and no deductions, each of those four schedules leaves about $34,330 for the year. One week of it is about $660. One month of it is about $2,861. Same year, different payday.

The estimate breaks when the hours do not match the schedule. Forty hours marked monthly is treated as a thin year. Eighty hours marked weekly is treated as constant overtime. Pick the schedule on the stub, then enter the hours on that one check. This is an estimate for planning, not a pay stub and not tax advice.
